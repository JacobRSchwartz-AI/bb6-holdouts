Halt

On tail-clock sweep 3·2279 exactly, the last carry walks the full length of the counter, finds no fuel, falls off the deep end of the written tape in state C, and the next cell puts D over a blank — the rule that was never written.

The 170-digit step count is the conjectured exact value — an exact per-sweep cost law, validated to the step on two miniature odometers. What the Coq proof certifies, with zero axioms, is the halt itself: Theorem odometer_halts : halts tm c0.

The Story

I · Genesis

354,540 steps from blank tape: the machine writes its scaffolding — a fuel reservoir of ~206 spare blocks — and parks at the base anchor.

II · The Count

One sweep = +1. Walk out to the edge, commute home over the tail, dip into the counter, carry, toggle out. The tail grows a block each sweep, so life costs ≈ 8ν² steps.

III · The Eras

When all wheels read F the counter overflows: it eats a fuel block and grows a new wheel. 549 overflow events, 71 wheels — each era 16× longer than the last.

IV · The Crisis

The reservoir runs dry. Deciders saw an immortal counting loop; the flaw only fires once per era, at a boundary no simulation ever reached.

V · Death

The final carry finds nothing to eat, walks off the written tape, and lands D on a blank: no rule. Halts after ≈6.79×10169 steps.
